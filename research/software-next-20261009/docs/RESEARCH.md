## Diagnosis of the failed baseline

The nine-tile 10× analysis produced 79 nominal candidates. The operator inspected these and rejected the result as tape, junk, noise and tiny regions. That is a failed real-data validation. Whole-field registration of three revisits confirmed the field of view, not the existence or identity of the outlined object. The original synthetic rectangle tests tested code behavior only.

The failure had a concrete structural cause: a Gaussian mixture trained on all foreground pixels determined both mask boundaries and presumed flake populations. Noise, illumination residuals and tape could define mixture components. Selecting the two components nearest substrate then promoted the weakest, least reliable regions. An 8 µm² lower area bound was also inappropriate for the intended devices.

## Experimental classical proposal design (retained for comparison)

1. Retain linear raw RGB and immutable stage/pixel calibration. Estimate the exposed-substrate surface and a separate high-frequency noise level. Measured flat/dark references remain preferable to a fitted surface; the current fitted correction is not called a measured flat field.
2. Use connected foreground as a proposal envelope, then refine its boundary with GrabCut, selecting a deep high-contrast interior seed automatically and background outside the envelope. This avoids counting optical halos as useful area. [Rother, Kolmogorov and Blake (2004)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/grabcut-interactive-foreground-extraction-using-iterated-graph-cuts/) describe the underlying local color/graph-cut method. Local color domains remain attached to the parent contour.
3. Require at least 100 µm² area and an interior diameter of at least 5 µm. These are configurable physical dimensions and therefore carry across objective changes. This is a practical initial screen, not a statement that every 100 µm² flake is useful.
4. Require evidence across the actual outline: paired inside/outside samples must show a localized color step above measured noise. Reject diffuse illumination hills even if a threshold happens to make a compact blob.
5. Use the published learned solidity/perimeter discriminator for shape, plus measured boundary localization, interior texture and within-domain spread. Edge-orientation concentration is retained as a descriptor, not a mandatory crystal rule. Save rejection reasons. These signals cannot uniquely distinguish all residue from crystals.
6. Fit local full-covariance mixture domains within each parent object so a small terrace is not absorbed into a scene-wide tape cluster. Retain large domains as `terraces` and evaluate their own boundaries/interiors; a useful terrace can qualify a parent attached to bulk material. Color groups do not establish layer number. Rank by physical size; no nearest-substrate priority remains.
7. Use independent material calibration only after object review. Keep acquisition-context matching, out-of-distribution rejection and an explicit unclassified outcome.

## Evidence required before claiming a working flake detector

A good test contains focused 20× images with several clearly visible, usefully sized real flakes plus tape, dust, bare substrate and chip-edge fields. Manually traced object/terrace masks establish the comparison; suspected monolayers remain a separate, unconfirmed class until reference characterization. Hold out entire fields or acquisition runs, not randomly sampled pixels from the same flake. Report object precision, recall, mask IoU, usable-area error and false candidates per mm². A repeated acquisition should reproduce the object boundary and local contrast, not merely match nearby scenery.

An initial strict version abstained on the failed 10x data but also rejected real public positives, so abstention was not accepted as success. Local terrace segmentation recovered 4 of 10 annotated >=100 square-micrometre regions at IoU>=0.5 across 20 public MoSe2 development images; 6 of 10 had a matching mask before quality rejection. This sensitivity is inadequate and is recorded as a limitation. Graph-cut boundary refinement is evaluated separately in `data/public-positive-graphcut.json`. Bulk objects are not fully annotated in that dataset, so unmatched proposals do not directly measure generic-object precision.

The visible elongated crystal in 10x tile 8 is a separate, manually located positive control. ROI-assisted GrabCut with explicit seed [617,1730] gives a 1599.6 square-micrometre contour following the crystal body. `data/reference-crystal-10x` records the ROI, seed, mask and original raw provenance. This is not an unseeded detection success or a monolayer assignment.

## Acquisition and hardware references

[ZEISS ZEN API documentation](https://github.com/zeiss-microscopy/OAD/tree/master/ZEN-API) and the installed protobuf contracts govern acquisition and stage control. The adapter attaches to the running supervised API session; it does not home or reconfigure the microscope. The nosepiece is encoded but not motorized: objective metadata must be read after a manual change and magnification-specific pixel/stage calibration must be checked before scanning or revisiting.

Autofocus combines noise-suppressed green-channel gradients, spatial aggregation, coarse peak confidence and fine curvature/noise checks. A focus score is acquisition evidence and cannot validate a flake mask. Chip identification assumes separated, approximately rectangular chips distinguishable from their holder; a grid is optional, and a missing chip does not shift later grid addresses.


## Thin-target screening update

[Biswas et al. (2023), Methods and supplementary Figure A6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03720) explicitly use 285 nm SiO2/Si and report roughly 7% optical contrast per MoTe2 layer before AFM confirmation. Their pictured monolayers darken the red/green appearance relative to the substrate. `mote2_thin_screen` uses this as a broad ranking prior, records its reference, and leaves layer number unknown. The numerical acceptance width is an engineering tolerance, not the paper's confidence interval. Saturated bright crystals are outside this thin-material screen.

Regional contrast is measured against a local substrate annulus where possible. Its reported standard error uses robust intensity spread and an effective independent-sample area of pi square micrometres; it describes spatial sampling error only, not systematic substrate/optics uncertainty.

The public MoSe2 dataset annotates one- and two-layer terraces. After graph-cut refinement, the first ten held-out fields contain eight >=100 square-micrometre annotated terraces: four have an IoU>=0.5 proposal, but only one passes the filters. This is a failed thin-sensitivity check, not a successful detector validation. Increasing smoothing to 0.6 micrometres did not improve the first five fields. Preserve these diagnostics; do not infer that an empty shortlist means no thin flakes exist. A learned instance model should be evaluated against this same positive control before deployment claims.

## Primary learned proposal backend

The optional MaskTerial runtime is now the default proposal backend when installed. It produces instance masks independently of the RGB layer catalog. The original learned masks survive intact in compressed NPZ files; source TIFF, sidecar metadata, model checkpoint, conversion code and mask hashes establish provenance. The original source image dimensions determine coordinates and physical area. Camera-output RGB from the preserved TIFF, rather than the gamma-transformed model input, determines local substrate contrast. Full camera radiometric linearity has not been established; see the radiometry audit below.

`analyze_scan` accepts `backend: auto`, `learned`, or `classical`. `auto` uses the installed learned runtime for real data, and the classical comparison for explicitly marked simulator data. `learned.predictions` accepts a saved predictions JSON or list of JSONs, so inference need not repeat. Without saved predictions the isolated runtime is invoked with logged, explicit input normalization. A failed learned run raises an error rather than silently substituting the experimental detector. See `external/maskterial-hf/README.md` for the isolated runtime and conversion checks.

Learned proposals are filtered only for requested physical size (default 100 square micrometres and 5 micrometres width), the configured upper area limit, and wholly outside-chip location. Disconnected components are measured separately; holes are retained and exact mask pixels determine area. The failed boundary, texture and shape gates do not veto learned proposals. Thin-screen compatibility ranks candidates but never establishes a layer count or suppresses out-of-prior learned masks. Fields lacking successful autofocus are marked focus-limited; even a passing autofocus does not claim sensitivity to thin flakes.

On the same first ten public MoSe2 test images, the generic synthetic MaskTerial model matched 7 of 8 annotated useful-size thin regions at IoU >= 0.5, with 14 proposals in total. This demonstrates substantially better positive-mask recovery than the experimental classical path on this limited benchmark. It does not validate target-domain precision or MoTe2 layer identification. The backbone conversion maps all 557 model tensors and gives identical intermediate ResNet outputs in an independent frozen-batchnorm comparison; original full-decoder parity has not been independently established. Live MoTe2 evaluation remains separate and must retain negative results as well as positives.

The source-code input audit found no missing substrate-color normalization before the shape model: it consumes BGR images with checkpoint mean `[107,148,85]` and standard deviation `[33,34,40]`. The authors' `model_trainer.py` pretraining augmentation includes channel shuffling/dropping and white-balance variation. The [synthetic generator](https://github.com/Jaluus/MaskTerial-Synthetic-Data) also varies background color through white balance. Thus the pale-green local substrate is not by itself evidence of a correctable normalization bug. Matching it arbitrarily to the checkpoint mean would be an unvalidated domain adaptation, not an established preprocessing requirement. In the first seven live 20x fields, both the generic and MoSe2-specific checkpoints produced no masks above the fixed 0.3 proposal threshold; this transfer failure is retained without threshold tuning.

## Separate empirical review proposals

`backend: empirical` retains the current contrast-foreground and automatic GrabCut masks, then requires useful physical area/width only. The strict detector remains `backend: classical`. Boundary support, shape, texture, border clipping and uniformity are measured and preserved as `evidence_warnings`; they do not veto empirical proposals. Strict dispositions/reasons are also retained. This intentionally permits ambiguous tape, haze or debris into a review list and must not be reported as a confirmed-flake detector.

Useful local optical terraces remain attached to their larger parent. Soft MoTe2 contrast compatibility ranks the parent or a useful terrace, with the selected region and contrast explicitly identified; material and layer remain unclassified. No useful thin region is removed merely for lying outside the literature contrast prior. The default minimum area is 100 square micrometres and minimum usable interior width is 5 micrometres.

Empirical analysis writes the regular raw review gallery plus `contact-sheets/raw-NNN.jpg`, separate `outlines-NNN.jpg` files, and a ranked `contact-sheets/index.json` linking every displayed crop to its raw source, source-pixel crop bounds and stage position. Raw sheets have no mask overlays. Contact sheets show the first 144 proposals at most; the gallery and index count make that display limit explicit. Focus-limited fields remain marked. The proposal-generation threshold and the absence of labeled target-domain controls still limit recall; removing the failed quality vetoes is not evidence of target-domain accuracy.

Applying empirical physical-size screening to the unchanged saved public-test masks recovers 2 of 8 annotated thin regions at IoU >= 0.5 (`data/public-positive-empirical.json`), compared with zero accepted by the strict rules on those same masks. This postfilter comparison isolates the effect of relaxing the vetoes; it does not improve mask-generation recall. Use this mode to produce inspectable candidate crops while gathering local controls, not to claim reliable thin-flake completeness.

The existing twenty-field 20x scan yields 52 empirical proposals after overlap deduplication, with four size rejects. Only one selected terrace meets the broad thin-contrast screen (about 107 square micrometres in tile 12); its curved, diffuse appearance is not a convincing thin crystal. Visual inspection of the first contact sheet shows predominantly residue/tape and bright bulk fragments. These counts are review workload, not confirmed-flake yield. The saved review is `data/20x-search-01/empirical-review`.

## Whole-image bulk-color reference and complete source overlays

The operator identified a real, bright bulk-flake population in R2C1 at approximately -0.8 to -0.5 on both red and green contrast axes, with contrast defined as `(substrate - object) / substrate`. These are explicitly bulk positive controls, not useful thin targets. The new `backend: bulk_reference` classifies the normalized raw pixels across each entire source field. It does not require a previous foreground proposal, contour or catalog entry. The selected R/G rectangle provides material-interior seeds; blue is recorded but is not used as an unrequested selection axis. Connected optical-mixture support extends each seeded region to the half-height contrast boundary. Spatially separated components remain separate, and holes remain holes.

Mask extraction and useful-size selection are separate. The configurable `min_detection_area_um2` defaults to 0.25 square micrometres (about nine source pixels at this 20x calibration). Every resulting source observation is retained in `observations.json` and in a full-resolution uint16 instance-label image, including objects below 100 square micrometres and repeated observations in overlapping fields. The deduplicated catalog retains the separate 100 square-micrometre useful-size screen. A downsampled, floating-point R/G contrast map accompanies each tile so the viewer can highlight a user-selected pixel population independently of either the catalog or saved detector acceptance.

The unchanged twenty R2C1 source fields yield **504 per-field bulk-reference observations**, of which **497 are below the catalog size screen**. Seven per-field size-qualified observations reduce to five physical catalog objects after overlap deduplication. The five-object catalog count is not the count of visible flakes and does not demonstrate complete flake recall. Earlier size filtering and catalog-only overlays concealed many small detections and duplicate source views. The new output is `data/chip-batch-01/R2C1/analysis-bulk-reference`; the original analysis is preserved. The exact masks and a raw-versus-all-mask comparison for four representative fields are in `data/segmentation-validation-v2`.

On those four fields, the direct whole-image color classification takes 0.4-0.8 seconds after 2-3 seconds of shared background normalization. A single classical comparison on tile 0 took 4.095 seconds for segmentation alone. These are single-machine development timings, not a throughput guarantee.

This reference mode establishes inspectable recovery of one user-selected optical population. It does not classify faint material, discover every color of crystal, or identify monolayers. Components without pixels in the supplied R/G seed rectangle are not part of this reference group. Dust sharing the same selected colors can still enter it. Fitted substrate correction remains a source of systematic contrast error; the exported raw TIFFs and local measurements remain available for inspection.

## Connected local-color prototype: failure retained

The separate `backend: empirical_v2` prototype replaces pooled GrabCut envelopes with connected local full-covariance color domains, anchored to exposed substrate. It retains exact holes and prevents disconnected pieces from being counted as one filled contour. On two known dust failures in R2C1, this exposes the separate granules and surrounding optical halos instead of the previous large, filled envelope. However, it also oversegments halos into many rings and small patches: 831 and 671 domains were generated in diagnostic runs, taking 221 and 80 seconds per field. Some broad halo domains remain; their physical-width screen prevents them becoming useful-size candidates, but that does not make their segmentation scientifically correct. This prototype remains explicit and experimental, is not the default, and is not a claimed repair of thin-flake sensitivity.

Targeted tests cover a faint two-percent-contrast region with an internal hole, adjacent distinct-color domains, selection on R/G without an invented blue veto, and retention of small/overlapping observations independently of catalog deduplication. These are code-behavior controls, not target-domain accuracy measurements. At that stage, the full suite passed 173 tests. Local, independently reviewed thin-MoTe2 masks are still required to measure thin recall and precision.

## Fast whole-field color domains: inspection backend

`backend: global_color_domains` fits one full-covariance model to sampled normalized pixels from the entire field, with a mixture of ordinary density sampling and color-cell sampling to preserve rare colors. It does not select samples from old foreground objects. A measured substrate distribution is anchored separately, and connected components of each non-substrate population retain holes and exclusive pixel ownership. The configured physical smoothing scale is shared with the normal contrast preparation. There is no per-object GMM, GrabCut, or hard shape/texture veto. The fitted populations, covariance matrices, sample budget and runtime are exported per tile; they describe optical colors, not materials or layer counts.

On R2C1 fields 0, 3 and 5, the standalone color segmentation takes 3.4-5.2 seconds per field. A complete three-field run including two-pass normalization, region measurements, 4,860 source observations and portable gallery takes 67.2 seconds. Nineteen domains pass the catalog area/width screen. The raw gallery shows that all three soft thin-screen matches are diffuse near-edge regions, not convincing thin platelets. This is a failed candidate-precision check, retained openly. The two known dust envelopes now expose separate particles/color regions, but some broad halos remain catalog-sized optical domains. The output is `data/segmentation-validation-v2/R2C1-subset/analysis-global-color`; it is an inspection/comparison backend and is not promoted to a reliable flake classifier.

The global-mode tests explicitly erase the old foreground mask before classification: a faint 2% terrace with a hole remains above 0.85 mask IoU, and disconnected equal-color particles remain separate instances. Focus metadata tests additionally distinguish verified surface interpolation from local autofocus and retain a focus-limited flag for extrapolation, mismatched surfaces or incorrect capture Z. Before the component repair below, all 188 repository tests passed; they do not replace local microscopy annotations.

## Traced component-deletion bug and direct repair

The independent raw-image audit selected cyan platelet P1 in R2C1 tile 0, without consulting detections. Tracing the original algorithm shows that its signal was present: a 264 square-micrometre initial foreground envelope contained P1 and a neighboring bright white object. GrabCut segmented both into separate foreground components. P1's component was 664 pixels (19.76 square micrometres), but `_refine_objects` retained only the component containing its single strongest seed, which lay in the neighboring white object. P1 was therefore explicitly deleted before the later useful-area filter. The issue was not absent contrast or failed normalization.

The shared refinement path now retains every independently connected foreground component supported by its input envelope, with exclusive instance labels and exact holes. No outer contour is filled, and disconnected pieces are not unioned into one instance. The detection floor is separate from the 100 square-micrometre catalog size screen. The later blanket morphological opening is removed from source masks because it shortened narrow features and could delete small pieces; physical width still controls catalog qualification. Each envelope receives a deterministic OpenCV RNG seed so retaining additional earlier envelopes cannot silently change later GrabCut initialization. All classical/empirical source observations are now exported before size filtering and overlap deduplication.

The final deterministic P1 mask has 0.892 IoU and an area of 19.64 square micrometres against the independent approximate visual polygon, compared with zero in the original source mask (the earlier pre-determinism diagnostic gave 0.895). The global-color experiment instead splits it across six optical labels: pooled coverage is 100%, but its dominant region reaches only 0.352 IoU. This illustrates why pixel recovery and coherent object recovery must be measured separately. P2/P3, neighboring cyan/white platelets, remain fused by GrabCut; the repair does not claim to solve touching-instance separation. Their example and a disconnected aggregate negative are recorded in `data/manual-visual-audit-01/benchmark.json` and `mask-coverage.json`; `P1-component-repair.jpg` provides a magnified raw/before/after comparison.

The supplied white-bulk color box is independently incomplete as a generic crystal detector: P1's interior normalized contrast is approximately `[+0.33,-0.62,-0.36]`, and P2's red contrast is approximately -0.07. Both lie outside a red/green rectangle bounded by -0.8 and -0.5, despite visibly strong signals. The bulk reference remains a separate known-color control, not the general scan classifier.

An investigated dust-background hypothesis was not supported: none of the old aggregate mask's 14,038 pixels belonged to a conservative one-sigma substrate distribution estimated from its local annulus. The apparent gaps have measurable haze/residue contrast. No unverified hard background seeds were imposed to erase those pixels. Retaining all components fixes a real deletion bug but does not establish material-specific precision. Five focused component/export tests pass after this repair; the earlier 188-test suite predates it. No additional broad validation suite was run during this focused debugging step.

With deterministic initialization, aggregate N1 remains one broad region of approximately 471 square micrometres, versus 417 in the old saved analysis. A transient, initialization-dependent diagnostic split it into smaller regions; that result is not counted as an improvement. The final comparison JSON and image use deterministic masks. The component repair is therefore a demonstrated recall fix for P1, with an openly retained dust-precision failure. No general flake-accuracy or monolayer claim follows from it.

The complete 20-field R2C1 output is `data/chip-batch-01/R2C1/analysis-component-repair`. It retains 3,688 per-field source observations, with 21 size-qualified catalog proposals after four duplicate observations are removed. All 20 fields carry verified local-autofocus or measured-surface provenance. The run took 445 seconds with two OpenCV threads, including 3,704 JPEG previews. Inspection of the raw contact sheet finds that all four soft thin-screen matches are still dust/haze aggregates in tiles 5, 9, 3 and 10; none is a convincing thin platelet. These outputs demonstrate a repaired component-loss bug and improved source visibility, not a useful thin-flake yield. Original analyses remain intact.

The four junk matches exposed a separate ranking bug: empirical mode changed their disposition to a physical-size-qualified optical proposal, and the thin-color ranking then ignored the already recorded strict rejection. Every one failed the existing uniformity and texture checks; tile 9 also touched the boundary. `assign_mote2_screen` now preserves the optical color prior separately as `color_compatibility`. A parent or terrace can enter the thin shortlist only when its existing strict object evidence passed; no thresholds were added or changed. Failed objects remain visible as `color_match_only`, with a zero shortlist score and retained numerical compatibility/rejection reasons. The saved dataset was relabeled and its gallery regenerated without rerunning segmentation: there are now **zero thin-shortlist entries**, while five catalog objects have some color-only compatibility (the fifth contains an additional quality-failed terrace). The 21 optical proposals and all source masks remain available.

Analysis also replays the new full-chip plane methods from their exact per-tile measured anchors and saved options. Both bounded extrapolation and interpolation must reproduce the recorded prediction and fit within the recorded controller Z tolerance. Accepted plane prediction remains explicitly different from locally verified focus or validated thin-flake sensitivity. Six focused semantic/focus tests pass for these updates; no old acquisition metadata was rewritten.

## Reference-based material-color selection on fixed masks

`material_selection.py` now separates color-reference selection from segmentation. It leaves every saved polygon, hole and instance-label image unchanged. The compact local profile uses five size-qualified examples from the user-identified bright bulk population, independently outlined cyan P1/P2, and the four reviewed dust/haze false thin proposals. Positive optical means in normalized R/G/B contrast are approximately `[-0.782,-0.620,-0.801]` (bright bulk), `[+0.323,-0.616,-0.368]` (cyan P1) and `[-0.079,-0.613,-0.545]` (cyan P2). The three negative mixture means are approximately `[+0.068,+0.031,+0.058]`, `[+0.337,+0.240,+0.274]` and `[+0.604,+0.691,+0.606]`. These measured populations distinguish the local white/cyan branches from the illustrated haze/dark debris; none is assigned a layer number.

Full RGB covariance, source-image and mask hashes, manual reference polygons, representative interior statistics and local-annulus medians are recorded in `material-profile.json`. The final initial profile id is `rgb-80a60e8f785a1439`. Application requires the matching sample/optical context and matching raw-frame camera job, exposure/white-balance resources, objective position, illumination and white level. Gamma-scaled previews are never used for classification. This is the reference-catalog approach supported by the earlier RGB-density literature, with an explicit residue/debris model rather than automatically interpreting arbitrary clusters as material.

Pixels must lie within a positive branch's 3D covariance ellipsoid and have at least a 3:1 maximum Gaussian density ratio over the negative/background groups. The ellipsoid cutoff is 11.345 squared Mahalanobis units (the Gaussian 99% three-dimensional value); the 3:1 ratio and occupancy settings are declared engineering parameters, not calibrated posterior probabilities. At least 60% of the eroded object interior must support a positive branch for a `material_like` result. Exclusion from the reference-selected view requires at least 60% debris-like interior and no more than 10% positive support. Other objects remain `uncertain`. Chemical identity stays unconfirmed for every result. Positive red/green direction is retained as a qualitative faint-unknown flag, without a hard blue-sign constraint or numerical monolayer assignment.

Selection uses measured supported pixels, not the whole parent area or a single matching speck. The useful-size screen requires a connected supported component of at least 100 square micrometres. Disconnected bright fragments cannot pool their areas. In the fixed R2C1 catalog, 8 of 21 optical proposals resemble the positive reference branches, 4 resemble the reviewed debris populations, and 9 remain uncertain; only **4** meet the connected supported-area screen. All four known false thin proposals are excluded from the selected-material view. P1 remains visible as a cyan reference-like source observation, with 98.5% interior occupancy and 13.66 square micrometres of supported pixels, below the useful catalog size. Across all source observations there are 227 reference-like, 1,443 uncertain and 2,018 debris-like observations; these are per-field observations, not unique physical flakes.

The selection is saved separately at `data/chip-batch-01/R2C1/analysis-material-selected`, with `selection-comparison.jpg` showing all 21 catalog proposals. The source dataset id and masks are preserved. The four excluded examples were included in profile construction, so their removal is a reference-consistency check, not a held-out precision benchmark. Unknown thin material can overlap debris colors; the current profile neither establishes thin-flake recall nor confirms material chemistry. Four focused checks cover disconnected-speck area pooling, overlapping positive/negative populations remaining uncertain, faint out-of-reference positive R/G colors, and optical-context mismatch. No new segmentation or broad test suite was run for this selection step.

## Offline camera radiometry and illumination audit

This audit used saved files and primary documentation only while the operator used the microscope manually. No camera connection or hardware setting was changed. `data/radiometry-audit/camera-radiometry-evidence.json` records source hashes, exact persisted values, job controls and numerical examples.

The current camera-only job explicitly sets exposure, white balance, color mode and zero white-balance offset, and disables auto exposure, automatic white balance and HDR. It does **not** set gamma, gain, color saturation, color profile, noise filtering or sharpening. The installed camera initialization file (`C:/ProgramData/Carl Zeiss/ZENCore/ServiceInitializationSettings.config`, section `Axiocam305c_S1329`) contains `CameraGamma=1`, `ApplyCameraProfile=false`, `NoiseFilter=false`, `UnsharpMask=false`, `AnalogGainModeList=0`, `ColorSaturation=0.5`, and camera LUT values `0`/`1`. These are saved initialization values, not live readback or per-frame historical evidence. The saturation/gain/LUT numeric conventions remain unverified; 0.5 must not be called half saturation. The exact installed ZEN core 3.12 manual, pp.865–870, confirms that interpolation, saturation, gain and acquisition postprocessing can affect the acquired pixels.

The ZEN streaming contract defines `enable_raw_data=false` as assembling complete frames instead of delivering partial lines. It is not a gamma switch. Our decoder and TIFF writer only rearrange BGR/RGB channels and preserve integer values. JPEG previews separately apply a power of 0.6. ZEISS distinguishes display curves from acquired pixel data, while warning that ordinary image export can bake display adjustments into pixels; our direct TIFF writer is not that ZEN export path. A 16-bit TIFF container and 12-bit values alone do not establish radiometric linearity. [ZEISS Axiocam Family brochure, p.69](https://asset-downloads.zeiss.com/catalogs/download/mic/1d062a0f-8560-45c9-b7cc-6085d6e66e1e/EN_solutions-brochure_axiocam_family.pdf)

For physical contrast, preserve gamma 1, fixed neutral/default saturation and fixed color interpolation, with HDR, adaptive enhancement, noise filtering and unsharp masking off. Gamma is appropriate for viewing, but changes measured contrast: a true 7% darkening becomes 3.21% after a power-0.45 transform. No verified job-resource binding for these additional camera controls was found, so guessed XML parameters have not been added. Existing typed job resources verify only exposure and white-balance resource round trips. At the next hardware handback, an eight-image stationary check is sufficient to start: two dark images, then repeated exposures at T/4, T/2 and T with fixed light/WB. Measure dark-subtracted response and temporal difference noise in unchanged unsaturated patches, and test whether local object/substrate contrast stays constant. This is a compact response check, not EMVA certification.

White balance must not be optimized by making the colored SiO2 substrate white. The saved 100 ms response experiment shows why the actual pipeline matters: changing only the third vendor coefficient from 1.3062 to 2.6124 changed median RGB from `[438,457,822]` to `[861,974,752]`. It is not a simple isolated blue multiplication. The fixed 266.6 ms profile has measured relative repeat fluctuations of approximately `[3.95%,1.70%,1.95%]` in R/G/B. These include any inter-frame optical variation and are not a calibrated electron read-noise measurement. Green currently provides the strongest measured temporal precision. Increasing digital brightness or WB gain does not create photons; gain generally trades headroom for representation, whereas increased collected photons improves shot-noise-limited SNR. [EMVA 1288 Linear 4.0, section 2.6](https://www.emva.org/wp-content/uploads/EMVA1288Linear_4.0Release.pdf)

The fixed profile reaches green's 99.9th percentile of 3879/4095 and clips about 0.047% of all green pixels. This is a useful exposure compromise for substrate/thin-region imaging, not proof that bright bulk colors are measurable. Saturated object pixels need a lower-exposure revisit or exclusion from color fitting. Lowering the lamp previously changed channel ratios unequally, so keep lamp power fixed within each optical profile and optimize exposure against red/green contrast-to-noise plus channel clipping. The 5500 K preset alone was not an improvement: the saved comparison gave 11.8% relative red fluctuation versus 6.45% for the previous WB at the same 100 ms. These were sequential measurements, not a controlled photon-transfer comparison.

For reflected brightfield, center Köhler illumination, set the field stop just outside the camera field, and begin with the aperture about two-thirds to four-fifths of the objective pupil diameter. The field stop reduces stray-light contrast loss; aperture sets an angle/resolution/contrast tradeoff and must be recorded rather than used as an untracked brightness control. No motorized aperture/filter control was established from the inspected active MTB configuration. [ZEISS Axioscope manual, reflected-light setup](https://asset-downloads.zeiss.com/catalogs/download/mic/2ee6619c-44de-4839-a0cb-07742507d434/EN_IM_Axioscope_5-7_V02.pdf), [ZEISS field-stop guidance](https://www.zeiss.com/microscopy/en/resources/insights-hub/foundational-knowledge/field-stop-diaphragm-function.html)

At 20×/0.40 and 550 nm, conventional Abbe/Rayleigh estimates are 0.69/0.84 µm, versus the measured sampling near 0.1725 µm/pixel. Moderate binning/ROI acquisition could accelerate autofocus, but requires a separate validated acquisition path and should not silently replace full-resolution material images. Illumination angle also changes interference: using n(SiO2)=1.46, the extreme ray at NA 0.4 has oxide phase thickness equivalent to 274.1 nm at normal incidence for a physical 285 nm film; at illumination NA 0.267 it is 280.2 nm. This calculation illustrates why a normal-incidence theoretical color curve is not an exact camera prior; it is not an angle-averaged prediction. [ZEISS numerical-aperture definition](https://www.zeiss.com/microscopy/en/resources/insights-hub/foundational-knowledge/numerical-aperture-and-light-cone-geometry.html)

For the current 403-field plan, science exposure alone is only 107.4 seconds at 266.6 ms per field. Reducing it to 100 ms saves at most 67.1 seconds while reducing photon-limited SNR to roughly 61% at the same photon flux. Job startup, settling and focus-image count should therefore be addressed before sacrificing thin-flake signal for shorter exposures. Neither camera brightness optimization nor a literature color prior establishes local thin-MoTe2 detection accuracy.

## Thin-region proposals from connected color plateaus (offline, experimental)

The bright/cyan reference model is not a thin-material calibration. `thin_regions.py` therefore starts from the entire linear substrate-relative RGB image without those reference masks, a material color box, or the old absolute 1.5% foreground threshold. Its connected region-merging method uses measured full RGB noise covariance and pooled region means. The ordered adjacency/union-find design is inspired by [Nock and Nielsen, Statistical Region Merging (2004)](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/Labo/Frank.Nielsen/pdf/2004-StatisticalRegionMerging-PAMI.pdf), but the Gaussian pooled-mean predicate here differs from their published distribution-free predicate; their segmentation guarantee must not be attributed to this implementation.

Preserving all RGB dimensions and normalizing the substrate follow [Sterbentz et al. (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970966/) and [Uslu et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.14845v2). This implementation does not claim to reproduce either algorithm. The reason to keep material inference separate is also physical: [Jessen et al. (2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5913130/) model the illumination spectrum, detector response and interference stack when predicting RGB contrast. Digital display gamma is not a physical calibration or an improvement in information; it remains outside quantitative contrast processing. Channel gains cancel in normalized linear contrast only when acquisition remains linear, unclipped, offset-corrected and spectrally unchanged.

Pixels are averaged in approximately 0.7 micrometre blocks. Segmentation and measurement use separate native pixel partitions inside each block to reduce selection bias. These are not independent camera exposures: demosaicing and spatial noise correlation may remain. Adjacent connected regions merge when their mean difference is compatible with the measured Gaussian noise model. Local evidence is then measured against an annulus, with its eight-sector color variation retained as a systematic uncertainty rather than divided by the number of pixels. Exact local Weber contrast is `(mean_contrast - annulus_contrast)/(1 - annulus_contrast)`, with the full RGB covariance propagated through both Jacobians. Candidate statistics are descriptive and selection-biased, not calibrated discovery p-values.

Default source region area is 25 square micrometres for runtime; the useful-area flag remains 100. An explicit smaller option is available for diagnostic controls. No morphology closing, hole filling or spatially disconnected area pooling occurs. Boundaries have the reported block resolution, approximately 0.69 micrometres in this data. Output includes full-source labels, centroid, physical width, local RGB covariance, noise-normalized heterogeneity and fitted within-region color ramp. No new hard shape/texture threshold was added. A nearby annulus dominated by other material remains a known limitation.

Saved R2C1 fields 0, 3 and 5 took 2.89, 4.33 and 4.51 seconds respectively for the region method (substrate normalization adds approximately 0.3 seconds per field). They retained 23, 17 and 11 optical plateau proposals, including 10, 2 and 5 at least 100 square micrometres. None of those useful-size regions passed even a provisional two-standard-error positive-red and green-minus-red direction check; these counts are not thin-flake findings. Results are in `data/thin-regions-02/`.

A deliberately labeled synthetic detectability check injected 154.26 square micrometre constant-contrast patches into an actual saved substrate crop, retaining its noise/texture. R/G contrasts 1%/2%, 4%/7%, and 8%/13.5% yielded dominant-instance IoUs 0.893, 0.920 and 0.849; a 0.5%/1% patch was missed. Blue contrast was allowed to be negative. These are software sensitivity checks, not measured monolayer recall or independent physical calibration. A smooth R/G/B ramp ending at 4%/7%/1% produced zero retained proposals. Three targeted regression tests passed: faint mixed-sign plateau detection, disconnected-area separation and the local Weber denominator. The strict flat-plateau method fragments strongly varying cyan/bulk controls and is a thin-region proposal branch, not a replacement for the existing general object boundary extractor.

The offline implementation is `flakefinder.radiometry.assess_exposure_series(frames, exposures_ms, dark_frames=None, white_level=4095)`. Supply saved `Frame` objects (preferred for optical-context checks) or a consistent sequence of RGB arrays. It requires at least three distinct exposure levels with complete repeat pairs, uses fixed low-texture patch coordinates, and reports dark-subtracted affine response, descriptive power-law gamma, paired RGB temporal covariance, relative R-G noise including pair drift, clipping and a preferred exposure selected only from measured inputs. High-confidence translations above two pixels and inconsistent profile/exposure metadata are rejected. Low-confidence registration, unknown substrate identity, missing camera metadata and the assumption of an exposure-independent dark reference remain explicit. `linearity_confirmed` and `emva_certified` remain false: a passing compact ladder is conditional evidence, not sensor certification. Five focused synthetic checks passed, covering gamma 1 versus 0.5, correlated RGB noise, clipping, missing dark data and invalid/shifted series. No microscope images were acquired for these checks.

## Restore accepted feature geometry; measure contrast without resegmentation

The whole-field plateau experiment replaced the object extraction step the user had already accepted. `measure_existing_regions.py` restores the intended separation: it accepts an existing full instance-label image, never changes geometry, and returns a measurement/status for every label. Local substrate pixels must be valid, outside every known foreground instance and at least1µm from any foreground. Optional extra foreground can only exclude additional substrate pixels. Object interiors are eroded0.5µm for color measurement, without altering the original mask. Spatial1µm patch means supply a full RGB mean covariance; annulus-sector variation is retained as systematic background uncertainty. Exact local Weber contrast and both covariance Jacobians are used. Inadequate substrate, small area, or insufficient spatial samples are explicit measurement limitations, never deletion reasons. The default measurement floor is25µm²; all smaller labels remain returned with their status. Useful catalog size remains a separate decision.
